hive-bench preliminary v2-ce

Which model should drive an autonomous dev pipeline?

Hive carries a task through planning, implementation, pull request, and code review. This benchmark replays real, already-merged tasks through that full production workflow under different model configurations, then judges the final post-review diff against the merged reference.

What this benchmark measures

The corpus contains six completed tasks from the Hive repository. For each task, the source is rewound to the original base commit and the candidate receives the frozen task inputs, but never the reference patch. A candidate may use one model for every stage or split planning and execution between models. Every candidate then runs real Hive in an isolated runner: plan → execute → open PR → production review and fix loop.

The current board is complete at 36 of 36 generation cells: six candidates × six tasks. It is still labeled preliminary because each judge-cell pair has only one independent score sample and this corpus has no curated objective test gates.

How the scores should be read

  1. Two independent rulers: Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol score each final diff from 0–10. Sol was explicitly run at xhigh; Fable's reasoning effort was not exposed by its runner.
  2. Never average judges together: each judge gets a separate leaderboard because their calibration differs. Every matrix cell shows Fable / Sol in that order.
  3. Show family conflicts: when the judge and candidate share a model family, that row is marked same-family. The score remains visible, but it is weaker evidence than a family-disjoint judgment.
  4. Keep efficiency descriptive: wall time, reported tokens, and API-equivalent cost are shown where the underlying runner recorded them. Missing values are reported as unknown, never inferred.

Read the full methodology and limitations before treating small score differences as meaningful.

What came out

GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh leads both preliminary leaderboards: 6.917 by Fable and 6.183 by Sol. Its Sol judgment is same-family, while its Fable judgment is family-disjoint. Grok 4.5 is second under Fable at 6.333 and lands close to Opus under Sol (4.217 versus 4.250), while completing the recorded timed cells fastest at 27.3 minutes on average. Grok's token count and cost are unavailable from its runner and therefore remain unknown.

The mixed Opus-plan → Codex-execute configuration is also strong: second under Sol at 4.783 and third under Fable at 6.167. Both primary judge families touch that candidate, however, so its rows carry a same-family warning under both rulers. GLM 5.2 is the least expensive configuration with complete cost telemetry at an estimated $21.97 per task, but ranks last under Sol and fifth under Fable on this corpus.

These are scoped findings, not a universal model ranking. Six tasks from one Ruby/CLI project and one score sample per judge cannot establish broad superiority; they can show what happened when these exact configurations drove this exact end-to-end workflow.
